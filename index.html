Why we’re called MyMathSteps

Math is understood one step at a time.

Every skill stands on the ones before it: fractions lead to ratios, ratios to equations, equations to algebra. When a step is missing, it rarely shows right away. It shows up a year later, as a wall.

AI makes missing steps harder to see. It jumps straight to the final answer, so the homework looks finished either way. That’s why everything here is built around the steps. Every question comes with a worked explanation, results point to the step to practice, and the “Ask your child” questions check that your child can explain each step in their own words.

How the free check-in works

Ten minutes to a clearer picture.

  1. 1

    Pick the student’s grade

    Choose Grade 6 or Grade 7. Early in the school year, use the grade they just finished. No account needed.

  2. 2

    Answer 10 or 11 questions

    The student works without a timer and sees a short explanation after every answer. It takes about 10 minutes.

  3. 3

    Choose what to explore next

    Review the results together—as a family or at the start of a tutoring session. Tutors can use the check-in without signing in; a parent account is only needed if a family wants to save and track results.

The check-in is an independent practice tool—not a standardized test, placement exam, or diagnosis. Use it to decide what to explore next, not as a measure of mastery.

Who we are

Built by a math teacher and mathematician.

MyMathSteps was founded by a mathematician who has taught math at every level, from elementary school classrooms to university lecture halls.

In classroom after classroom, our founder saw the same pattern: students who hit a wall in algebra rarely lacked ability. They were missing a few skills from earlier grades that nobody had spotted in time. Today, with AI able to finish almost any homework problem, those gaps are even harder to see.

We believe core math skills matter more in the age of AI, not less. Number sense, proportional reasoning, and algebraic thinking are what let a person reason through a problem, check an answer, and notice when a tool is wrong. Every child can raise their math level when someone shows them exactly what to work on.
